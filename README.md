# Climate Claim on Trial

**A teaching prototype: a student can discover how selected years change a headline, and learn why a responsible analysis sometimes cannot give a verdict.** Its checks are simulation checks under stated assumptions; the procedure is frozen, not broadly validated.

*Investigate Earth-system trends with NASA data.* Team Earth on the Stand. NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, "Be An Earth System Trend Detective!" (Local Event: Arlington, Virginia, virtual).

## What it is

We examine specific trend claims for a stated measurement, place and period, using NASA Earth data. Each claim is written down before its data are downloaded. The page draws the yearly series and tests the trend with the tests the challenge names: a t-test, the Mann-Kendall trend test with the Sen estimate of the rate of change, and confidence intervals. Then it tests the tests, on simulated records where the right answer is known, to measure how often each one raises a false alarm. A short sequence of test steps follows, each changing one thing, and the page shows what each did to the answer. When its own checks on simulated data fail, the site withholds the verdict. Withheld describes this tool's limits; it does not establish that the claimed change is absent. You can also pick your own measurement, place and time period. The site is static files that run in your browser: no server, no account, no AI model at runtime.

## Three ways in

- **Investigate.** Your own question. Choose one of 22 NASA measurements: 12 from NASA POWER, asked for live (model output, so internet is needed), and 10 from a library of NASA satellite and model products that we precomputed and ship with the site (rainfall, water storage, land surface temperature, snow cover, air temperature, water vapor, haze, nitrogen dioxide, and two GLDAS model variables, soil moisture and evapotranspiration, labeled as model output). Pick a region and the years, and follow the five steps from the challenge brief. Results are labeled "your own exploration, not pre-registered", and the page counts how many questions you have tried, because many tries raise the chance of a false alarm.
- **Case files.** Six claims we wrote down before downloading each claim's data. Each gets a ruling, a map of every grid cell at four hearings (three on the two NO2 pages, where the fourth, the false-discovery step, has no validated cell test and is hidden), the regional series and the limits of the finding. Southeastern US, Sahel and Alaska also have a "Second witness" view: an additional record used to compare the result (exploratory, it never changes a ruling).
- **How it works.** The method in plain words, the False-Alarm Planet (a simulated world with no real trend, where many cells still look significant), a glossary, and a Data and provenance page with files, hashes and DOIs. The Home page opens with one NASA record under two time windows that give opposite headlines. We selected these two windows to illustrate how the estimated trend changes with the years. Both describe their selected periods; neither represents the full record.

At 21 years and memory 0.39 all seven candidate tests raise too many false alarms. Says nothing about causes or surface exposure. |

**What qualifies each result** (the same text is on each case-file page):

- **Alaska.** This is model output, not a measurement, and its box includes sea cells. KNMI/CRU TS station records (land only) give +0.36 per decade; station minus MERRA-2 is -0.06 per decade (p = 0.018), so MERRA-2 warms slightly faster than the stations.
- **Sahel.** The p = 0.030 is from the first test (plain Mann-Kendall) and p = 0.004 from the second (bootstrap Mann-Kendall, 499 resamples, so the smallest possible p is 0.002), which was chosen after we saw the first results. The first test was already valid, only cautious: the change bought power, not validity. 15.6% of the area was flagged under the first test. About the map's power: in 10 simulated map settings (25 seasons, cells that truly change by the reference amount) the map's test found 0.7% to 11.0% of those cells when every cell had the same memory level (0 to -0.7), and 16.9% to 23.9% in the two settings where memory differs from cell to cell. So a small flagged share is not evidence of a narrow change (`web/data/exploratory.json`, `sahel_map.map_power`). The result depends on this window's alternating wet and dry years: in an exploratory simulation it stops being significant at year-to-year memory 0.2 or more. IMERG blends changing satellite inputs and is gauge-adjusted, and the CRU station check shares many of those gauges. Both runs stay on record.
- **Southeastern US.** This case concerns summer daytime land surface temperature in the stated region and years. Its descriptive cooling slope does not establish a broader regional climate trend; the ruling is withheld. The comparison records do not show the same cooling: ESA LST CCI gives +0.37 per decade (2001 to 2024) and KNMI/CRU TS station maximum temperature gives +0.30 (2001 to 2025). None of the three slopes is significant. MODIS already trails station air temperature by about 0.5 °C per decade over 2001 to 2020, before the orbit drift.
- **Wisconsin.** Our test is conservative here (it catches 23% of a real rise of the reference size), so "not supported" means the record does not show the claimed rise at the registered bar. The map has 42 cells and finds almost nothing even when it is valid. Three smoke summers carry the rise in a reviewer's check (without 2021, 2023 and 2025 the slope is -0.002 per decade), Aqua's later overpass since 2021 is flagged in our library but not removed here, a plain t-test gives p = 0.04, and the sensitivity check is not stable.
- **Eastern US NO2.** The gate is blind to curved trends (see below): in a simulation of a clean curved decline like this one it gave a verdict in 0 of 2,000 runs. That is a weakness of the method, not a finding about NO2.
- **Northern India NO2.** Its record is not curved in the way the eastern US one is: a curve of its shape would get a ruling 67.7% of the time in simulation, and its memory (0.39) is above that simulation's 95th percentile (0.34). So its memory is not explained by this fitted curve alone. We do not know its source.

A ruling describes the data for that place and period. It does not say why anything changed and it is not a forecast. We describe the selection of a window or a test and what follows from it; we do not infer anyone's motive. Every sentence on a ruling comes from a fixed template.

**The NO2 pair (C-E1 and C-E2).** These regional NO2-column records illustrate opposite trend directions. They do not by themselves identify causes, measure surface exposure, or rank national climate performance. We registered these two claims together at 1:15 PM ET on Nov 14, 2026, before any NO2 data were downloaded, as an opposite-sign pair: NO2 over the eastern US has fallen, and over northern India has risen. Both came back withheld. The readings point the claimed way in both boxes, and the maps flag most of the area in that direction (descriptive only). But the record is only 21 years long and its year-to-year memory is high, so none of our validated tests can speak for it. We did not change the gate or the claims after seeing this. The gate is blind to curved trends, and that is a limit of the method. It measures memory around a straight line, so a trend that bends (steep early, flatter later, like the eastern US record) looks like memory, and the site withholds. We kept the withholding because the gate was frozen before these results, but it means the gate can refuse clearer evidence more often. In an exploratory simulation of a clean curved decline with no memory at all, the gate gave a verdict in 0 of 2,000 runs, 13% with twice the noise and 59% with four times: a cleaner signal is less likely to get a verdict. Northern India is different: its record is not curved in that way, and its memory is not explained by this fitted curve alone. In no-trend series with memory as high as 0.8, a trend this strong would occur only about 1.5% of the time (5.5% at 0.9, close to a random walk), so this series alone is not what establishes the eastern US decline; other evidence would be needed.

## Terra overpass witness check (exploratory)

The Southeastern US case has an extra panel. Terra's daytime pass drifted earlier: mean local solar view time was about 10:58 in 2001 to 2019 and about 9:47 in 2025. Changing observation time may affect the record. One rough adjustment for view time moves the slope from -0.35 to about +0.15 °C per decade; another gives about -0.10. Neither is significant. We show this as a witness check, not a result: year and view time are tangled together, the fitted interval is wide, it is labeled "exploratory check, not the registered test", and the ruling does not change. The drift does not explain the whole gap: against station maximum air temperature MODIS already falls behind by about 0.5 °C per decade over 2001 to 2020 (95% interval +0.24 to +0.90, p = 0.003), which may be a real difference between land surface and air temperature. Sources: `review/TERRA_DRIFT_SOURCES.md`.

## Run it locally

You need Python 3 and nothing else.

```
python web/tools/serve.py 8765
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8765/. The folder `web/` is the whole site, so any static host works. Case files work offline.

## How we kept it honest

- **Questions registered; methods amended and disclosed; final gate frozen.** Claims, regions, periods and the first test rules for Alaska, Sahel and the Southeastern US were committed at 10:29 AM ET on Nov 14, 2026 (commit 839f802); the Wisconsin claim was added at 10:35 AM ET (Addendum 1, commit 36de9c7). Each was committed before its claim data was downloaded. The text and its SHA-256 are in `event_record/`; the hash file has two lines, the Addendum 3 version and the current file after Addendum 4 (the one that matches today). The NO2 pair was registered at 1:15 PM ET the same day, also before its data were downloaded (Addendum 3).
- **Method changes, all logged.** We tried seven candidate tests on simulated series with known answers. Our first choice, Hamed-Rao, rejected too often when years resemble each other, so a rule fixed in advance picked the test for each series from simulations only, before any real result was run. Later, an outside review of the first bundles found three gaps in our checks: negative persistence was rounded up to zero, the skewness check measured persistence instead of skew, and missing years passed. We fixed them in a third round. Our log says the rules for that round were written before its simulations but after we had seen the second-round results; git cannot confirm that order (see the next bullet), so we disclose it as a change made after results (`event_record/WP0_CLAIM_REGISTRATION.md`, Addendum 4). It made the Sahel result stronger (p 0.030 to 0.004; the earlier test was already valid, so this bought power, not validity) and moved Wisconsin from "withheld" to "not supported". The earlier numbers and run identifiers stay on record, and we made no further rule changes after that.
- **What git can and cannot prove.** The first three claims (commit 839f802, 10:29 AM ET), the Wisconsin claim (commit 36de9c7, 10:35 AM ET) and the NO2 pair's pre-run plan (commit 1b101af, 4:43 PM ET, before its results in 4e6d168 at 5:12 PM) were committed before their results. The round 2 and round 3 rules were not: we wrote them in our log at 11:40 AM and 1:40 PM, but they first appear in git in the same commits as their results (1e93307 at 12:09 PM and 3152f39 at 2:51 PM). So git cannot show that those rules were written before the results they changed. That rests on our log, and the log's times were typed by the workers (several were corrected afterwards). The git commit times are the record. The post-result checks listed under Limits were logged before they were computed (commit 39a9580). Commit hashes changed when the public history was cleaned; see event_record/REGISTRATION_HASH_MAP.md.
- **Well-known claims, chosen on purpose.** Alaska warming, the Sahel rainfall recovery, the Southeastern US "warming hole", the challenge author's Wisconsin haze example and the US and Indian NO2 trends are topics with known answers, picked for teaching. Registration fixes the data, region, period and test before download, not whether the answer was already known.
- **Corrections and amendments we know of.** (1) The first run of the map rule did not follow the instruction we had written; we corrected it after the first Alaska run and kept both runs (`stats/out/C-C/bundle_firstrun_celltest_tfpw.json`). (2) The pass bar for bias in the Sen slope was relaxed before the Sahel, Southeastern US and Wisconsin runs. (3) A typo in our log gave the overall false-alarm limit as 0.047; the code always used 0.05 plus two standard errors. (4) The first run of the 21-year NO2 checks used the wrong Python installation; it was stopped, kept in a discarded folder and repeated. (5) Addendum 4 first called the Sahel round 2 test Yue-Wang; it was plain Mann-Kendall, corrected nine minutes later. (6) Several log header times were corrected to the git commit times. (7) Bugs found and fixed before anything shipped: a tie-variance error in Mann-Kendall, a double scale factor in haze data and a flipped NO2 grid.
- **Withheld verdicts.** When the simulated checks fail for a series, the page gives no ruling. Three of the six case files are withheld: the Southeastern US and the two NO2 regions.
- **What the simulations show.** Simulation checks show how each test performs under the stated scenarios. They do not show universal calibration, and they are not the probability that a particular claim is true.
- **Failed checks are published** in `stats/out/validation/`. `review/data_qa/` records an independent re-read of the data, which found no processing error.
- **Limits.** Everything below is also on the Method page ("Known limits and the checks made after the results"). Numbers marked exploratory were computed after the results, are not the registered test and change no ruling (`event_record/STATS_LOG.md`, entries of Nov 14 18:06 and 18:12 ET; `stats/scripts/exploratory_r3.py`; `web/data/exploratory.json`).
  - **Assumptions.** Our simulated checks assume one-year memory, bell-shaped or one-sided skewed noise and straight-line trends. They did not test long-memory noise, sudden steps, rare extreme years such as smoke summers, or curved trends. Long-memory noise would make every test raise more false alarms; curved trends make the gate withhold (see the NO2 pair). A real series' persistence is estimated from the same series, and with 21 to 25 years that estimate is rough. We do not test whether the whole pattern of cells could arise by chance.
  - **Sahel.** The result is significant under both tests (p = 0.030 and 0.004). In exploratory simulations of no-trend series, a pattern this strong happens by chance 0.05% of the time at year-to-year memory -0.7, 2.8% at 0, 4.1% at 0.1, 7.2% at 0.2 and 8.3% at 0.3, so it holds if this window's alternation is real and not if memory is 0.2 or more (a reviewer's 20,000-run check got 6.5% at 0.2). The registered method alone (Mann-Kendall with Hamed-Rao, no gate) gives p = 0.030 here. The map's test was checked at memory -0.7 and -0.5, but the individual cells have a middle memory of about -0.16 and a fifth are above zero, so the 22% may over-flag. IMERG blends changing satellite inputs (TRMM before 2014, GPM after, changing microwave sensors) and is calibrated to GPCC gauges; CRU uses many of the same gauges, so it is a cross-check, not an independent measurement.
  - **Registered method alone.** Without the gate, Mann-Kendall with Hamed-Rao gives p = 0.27 (Southeastern US), 0.030 (Sahel), 0.00085 (Alaska), 0.22 (Wisconsin), 0.0013 (eastern US NO2) and 0.000094 (northern India NO2). It agrees with the ruling in direction and significance for the three released cases; for the NO2 pair it would have called a significant trend, which the gate refuses to trust at 21 years and this memory, and for the Southeastern US it would have called none.
  - **Alaska.** Model output, not a measurement. Of simulated trendless series like Alaska's that received a verdict, 7.4% were called significant (4.8% counting withheld ones; the 5% bar we checked is the overall rate with withheld counted as no call). The 7.4% limits how far a released verdict can be trusted. A separate exploratory check, which is not the registered test, found that a pattern this strong would arise with no trend only 0.2% to 0.9% of the time at memory 0.1 to 0.3. Stations warm about 0.06 °C per decade slower (p = 0.018).
  - **Six claims.** Each claim was tested at 5% on its own, with no adjustment across cases. A Holm adjustment of the three released p-values is exploratory arithmetic for those three only; it is not protection across all six claims. It gives Alaska 0.004, Sahel 0.008 and Wisconsin 0.114; with the first Sahel test the Sahel figure would be 0.060 and would not pass 0.05.
  - **Footprints.** Some analysis cells reach past the registered boxes: the Wisconsin map uses 42 one-degree cells from 42 to 48 N and 93 to 86 W against a box of 42.5 to 47.1 N and 92.9 to 86.8 W (including parts of Lake Michigan and Lake Superior); the Alaska and eastern US NO2 boxes include sea cells (no land mask).
